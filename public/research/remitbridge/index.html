RemitBridge

A complete product and technical brief for a remittance middleware layer connecting banks in send markets with licensed payout rails in receive markets, starting with Saudi Arabia to Nigeria.

Architecture Brief v1.0 - March 2026 Founders & Tech Lead
01

Overview

What we are building and why

RemitBridge is positioned as an invisible infrastructure layer for cross-border transfers: fair FX, fast settlement, and no hidden cuts in the corridor experience.

The short pitch: a sender in Saudi Arabia uses a familiar banking interface, sees the real rate and fees upfront, and the recipient in Nigeria gets credited within seconds.

What it is

Middleware, not a bank

The platform coordinates instructions between licensed institutions instead of holding customer funds directly.

What it is not

Not a wallet or issuer

It does not issue cards or operate as the regulated institution of record; partners keep that responsibility.

Revenue model

FX spread plus fee

Revenue comes from a transparent spread on the locked FX quote and, where needed, a flat transaction fee.

Primary users

Migrant workers and corridor operators

The first audience is Nigerians in Saudi Arabia, with future expansion into diaspora, agents, and business payments.

02

Capabilities

Minimum system requirements

These are the core platform capabilities required for a credible launch rather than optional enhancements.

User registration and KYC

Collect profile details, ID documents, and liveness checks with automated verification.

Live FX display

Show the quote immediately, then lock the rate for a short confirmation window.

Outbound transfer flow

Capture the sender amount, payout amount, fees, and confirmed beneficiary in one path.

Instant payout credit

Push funds to Nigeria payout partners fast enough that the recipient sees a near-real-time credit alert.

Transaction history

Provide a full receipt trail for support, audit, disputes, and user trust.

Beneficiary management

Let repeat senders save recipients and re-use verified destinations with one tap.

Notifications

Use SMS and push for OTPs, debit confirmations, payout completion, and exception handling.

Admin operations

Expose internal controls for failures, suspicious activity, and manual overrides.

Reconciliation

Generate settlement files and ledgers across corridors at month end.

03

Onboarding

Sign up, verification, and KYC clearance

The experience needs to feel short to the user even though multiple compliance checks run behind the scenes.

1

Entry point

User opens the app or site and lands on a simple promise: send money home quickly and fairly.

2

Phone verification

Phone number and country are collected first, then confirmed through OTP delivery.

3

Profile details

Name, email, and date of birth are captured and later cross-checked against the identity documents.

4

Identity proof

User submits government ID plus a live selfie so the KYC partner can validate both authenticity and liveness.

5

AML and sanctions screening

Name screening and politically exposed person checks run in the background before account activation.

6

Activation and secure access

User sets a PIN, optionally enables biometrics, and lands on an empty but ready dashboard.

04

Transfer flow

Send and receive journeys

The transfer experience should feel confident and deterministic: clear price, clear recipient, clear finality.

Send money

Core sender flow

  • User enters the send amount and sees the live rate immediately
  • Recipient is selected or created, with name enquiry validating the destination
  • Confirmation screen exposes fee, rate, arrival expectation, and countdown timer
  • PIN or biometric approval gates the actual transaction request
  • Middleware locks the rate, debits the sender, and triggers Nigeria payout rails

Receive money

Recipient experience

  • Recipients do not need a RemitBridge account to receive into OPay, Kuda, or a bank account
  • If they are also users, they see richer receipt detail and can trigger future request-link flows
  • SMS and in-app notices close the trust gap on both sides of the corridor

05

Architecture

System blueprint

Every external dependency should sit behind an internal service interface so one partner outage does not take the platform down.

Client layer

Web and mobile experiences share the same product logic while hitting a central API gateway.

Core services

User, transaction, FX, beneficiary, notification, reconciliation, and audit services stay clearly separated.

Data layer

PostgreSQL stores the ledger state, Redis caches rates and sessions, and object storage keeps documents and receipts.

Compliance adapters

KYC, AML, and sanctions checks are delegated to specialist partners behind stable internal APIs.

Send-side connectors

Saudi bank integrations are treated as debit adapters rather than core product logic.

Payout connectors

OPay, Kuda, Moniepoint, and fallback routes can be swapped by the transaction engine.

Operating principle: if a payout rail fails, the transaction engine should retry another approved partner before escalating to manual review.

06

Integrations

External services and why they exist

Each partner below plays a defined role in KYC, pricing, debit orchestration, payout routing, or communication.

Service Category Why it matters How it is used
Smile Identity KYC / AML Validates Nigerian IDs, selfies, and identity matching. Called during onboarding, with async follow-up when needed.
Dojah KYC Cross-checks NIN and BVN details for Nigeria-specific identity confidence. Used as a synchronous verification layer inside onboarding.
Jumio or Onfido KYC Extends the model to international IDs such as passports and residence permits. SDK plus API callback for non-Nigerian users.
ComplyAdvantage Compliance Screens users and transfers against sanctions, PEP lists, and adverse media. Triggered at signup and on risk-based transaction thresholds.
XE Currency API FX rates Primary live pricing source for corridor quotes. Polled continuously and cached for quote generation.
Open Exchange Rates FX rates Fallback source for resilience and historical audit support. Activated when the primary feed is unavailable.
Saudi National Bank / Al Rajhi Send-side bank Debit the sender after authorization inside the Saudi corridor. Private bank API integrations with partnership agreements.
OPay Payout rail Primary instant credit route for recipients in Nigeria. Receives credit instructions and returns webhook confirmations.
Kuda Payout rail Secondary payout path for redundancy and account-specific routing. Used when OPay is unavailable or recipient preference dictates it.
Flutterwave Payout / verification Provides fallback routing and bank account name enquiry. Used heavily when adding new beneficiaries.
Termii and Twilio Communications Handle OTP and transaction messaging across Nigeria and international markets. Termii first, Twilio as fallback or non-Nigeria default.
Firebase Cloud Messaging Push Drives in-app alerts for completed or delayed transfers. Server-triggered notifications from the notification service.

08

Security

Protection, audit, and compliance posture

A remittance product becomes a security target early, so protections need to be structural rather than cosmetic.

JWT plus biometric approval

Short-lived access tokens and a fresh user-authentication step before each transfer.

Encryption everywhere

TLS in transit, managed keys at rest, and restricted access around KYC documents.

Rate limits and velocity rules

Suspicious volume and threshold breaches should be configurable without code changes.

Tiered limits

Transaction ceilings adapt to KYC depth, source-of-funds evidence, and risk appetite.

Immutable audit trail

Every login, override, and transfer transition is written to a durable event history.

Ongoing AML monitoring

Transaction behavior should continue to feed compliance checks after onboarding is complete.

09

Corridors

Launch path and geographic expansion

The architecture is corridor-agnostic, but every new market requires a send-side bank partner and a receive-side payout partner.

Launch

Saudi Arabia to Nigeria

SAR to NGN with SNB or Al Rajhi on the send side and OPay or Kuda on the receive side.

Phase 2

UAE to Nigeria

AED to NGN with a more open banking-friendly environment on the send side.

Phase 2

United Kingdom to Nigeria

GBP to NGN with open banking and a large diaspora base.

Phase 2

Saudi Arabia to Ghana

Same debit partners, new receive rails via mobile money and banks.

Phase 3

Saudi Arabia to Kenya

M-PESA gives the receive side a compelling mobile-money advantage.

Phase 4

United States to Nigeria

A large prize but a far heavier licensing and competition landscape.

10

Roadmap

Build sequence from zero to launch

The fastest way to ship is to get the corridor live in constrained phases instead of trying to build every market at once.

Phase 01

Foundation

  • Cloud infrastructure, auth, user service, and ledger foundation
  • KYC, AML, and sanctions integrations
  • FX service, notification service, and base admin tooling
  • Bank and payout sandbox access negotiations start immediately

Phase 02

Mobile MVP

  • Onboarding, KYC capture, dashboard, and send-money flow
  • Beneficiary management, receipts, and push notifications
  • End-to-end sandbox transaction testing

Phase 03

Corridor go-live

  • Move Saudi bank and Nigeria payout integrations into production
  • Run closed beta transfers and tighten reconciliation workflows
  • Complete security audit and app store submission

Phase 04

Expansion

  • Extend to UAE and Ghana corridors
  • Add international KYC and multi-currency presentation
  • Launch web dashboards and agent-led transfer tools
